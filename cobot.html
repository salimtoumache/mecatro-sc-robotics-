DH table (standard)

Frame i is attached to the axis of joint i+1. Each row is: Ai = Rz(θ) · Tz(d) · Tx(a) · Rx(α). Orange numbers are the current values. These are the real numbers Universal Robots publishes for the UR5: the negative a values mean the arm folds back along −x, and there is no offset on any θ.

DHM table (modified DH)

Frame i is attached to the axis of joint i. Each row is: Ai = Rx(αi−1) · Tx(ai−1) · Rz(θ) · Tz(d).

What is special about this DH table?

    
Compare it with the industrial 6-axis robot. Here rows 4 and 5 have d4 and d5 ≠ 0: the three wrist axes do
       not meet at one point. This is the typical "offset wrist" of Universal Robots. The wrist is compact and
       cheap to build (three identical small joints), but the inverse kinematics cannot split into "position by joints 1-3, orientation
       by joints 4-6" as simply as for a spherical wrist. Still, the UR5 has a closed-form solution with up to
       8 configurations (shoulder left or right × elbow up or down × wrist flipped or not); the page uses the
       numerical method to stay general.

    

      
Try it: three kinds of singularity

      
    
        
  • Wrist: set q5 = 0. Axes 4 and 6 are on the same line (compare z3 and z5 with the frame selector).
    • 
        
  • Elbow: set q3 = 0. The arm is fully stretched: it cannot move further away, and the tool speed along the arm is lost.
    • 
        
  • Shoulder: bring the wrist centre above axis 1. Then turning q1 hardly moves the tool.

A real cobot controller slows down or stops near these configurations.

How a cobot stays safe

    
The standards ISO 10218 and ISO/TS 15066 describe four ways of working next to a person:

    
    
      
  1. Safety-rated monitored stop: the robot stops when a person comes in, and restarts when they leave.
    2. 
      
  2. Hand guiding: the person moves the arm by hand, with an enabling device.
    3. 
      
  3. Speed and separation monitoring: a scanner measures the distance; the closer the person, the slower the robot.
    4. 
      
  4. Power and force limiting: contact is allowed, but force and pressure stay below the limits for each body part.
    5. 
    

    
Important: a cobot is safe only with its tool and its task. A sharp tool or a heavy fast load can make the whole cell
       dangerous again, so the risk assessment is done for the complete application, not for the arm alone.


    
Inverse kinematics: how the demo finds the joint angles

    
The demo says "put the tool at this point, pointing down" and the page finds the 6 joint angles.
       The solver starts from a typical UR "ready" pose (0, −90, 90, −90, −90, 0) and uses a numerical method:

    
1. compute where the tool is now (forward kinematics)
2. compute the error between the target and the tool
3. use the Jacobian to find the small joint changes that reduce the error
4. repeat until the error is below 0.01 mm

Where is a cobot used?

      
Small and medium factories with many different small batches: machine tending,
         screwdriving and assembly, pick and place, packing and palletizing light boxes,
         quality inspection with a camera, gluing, dispensing, polishing, laboratories
         (pipetting, sample handling), food and cosmetics, and even coffee shops. Payloads go from 3 to about 25 kg,
         with a repeatability around ±0.03 mm. Well-known makers: Universal Robots (UR3e to UR20), FANUC CRX, ABB GoFa and SWIFTI,
         KUKA LBR iisy, Doosan, Techman, Franka Emika (7 axes), Kinova.

      
End effector (tool)

      
Yes, a pinch gripper (pince) works, like the two-finger one in the 3D model; electric grippers such as
         the Robotiq 2F-85 are the most common choice. Because a cobot works near people, tools are chosen to be safe too: rounded
         fingers, limited gripping force. Other tools:

      
    
        
  • Vacuum suction cup, for boxes and flat parts.
    • 
        
  • Soft or adaptive gripper, for fragile or irregular objects (food, fruit).
    • 
        
  • Electric screwdriver, for assembly lines.
    • 
        
  • Glue or dispensing nozzle and small polishing or sanding tool.
    • 
        
  • Camera and force-torque sensor, for inspection and delicate insertion.
    • 
        
  • Quick tool changer: the same cobot does different jobs during the day.
